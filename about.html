Every process has a customer.That’s where we start.

Our experience runs across apparel and footwear, at one of South Africa’s biggest retailers and at a global fashion brand. It continues through homeware and textiles, and into consumer electronics across private label and global brands.

Much of it has been spent between head office and the shop floor: designing systems and processes that move the business forward without taking time from the people who serve customers.

We’re wary of either/or thinking. The cheaper option often does a job for the better one: it keeps a range within reach, or it shows the customer what the better option is worth. Sometimes the right call is to give up margin to grow volume and profit, and part of the work is making that case with the evidence.

Who receives this, and how does the change make their day better?

Our aim is to pay it forward: to create value for our clients, for the people they serve, and for the society we share.

What we hold to.Seven principles for work that lasts.

  1. 01

    We fit the work to the people.

    A process should take the shape of the people who run it, and of the customer who receives what it makes.

  2. 02

    Every workaround is evidence.

    The private spreadsheet, the extra check, the rule everyone quietly skips. Each one marks a place where the process stopped fitting.

  3. 03

    The process is the program.

    Instructions, whoever runs them, and what’s left when they’re done. We design all three, then decide what runs each step: people, software or AI.

  4. 04

    Automate the layer, hand back the seat.

    When routine work moves to a machine, the person moves up with their judgement intact. Nobody is left standing outside.

  5. 05

    Use what’s proven. Protect the why.

    Standard tools for the parts that are the same everywhere. The reasons behind your work stay yours.

  6. 06

    Build seats we can leave.

    The work isn’t finished until your team can run it, and change it, without us.

  7. 07

    Design for what doesn’t change.

    Tools come and go. What people need from their work mostly stays the same, so that’s what we build around.

Someone built the seat you’re sitting in. We build the next one.
